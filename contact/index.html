The fastest way to reach us is to call the dispatch line. It is answered 24/7, including nights, weekends, and holidays, and we do not use web forms or chat. A person on the line asks a few questions, gives you first steps, and sets up a visit.

Our office is at 226 North Avenue in Westfield, NJ 07090, and email goes to contact@247plumberwestfieldnj.com. Email is for non-urgent questions. If water is moving or a drain is backing up, call.

What to tell the dispatcher

A few details let us send the right person with the right tools.

  • Your street address and nearest cross street.
  • What is happening: leak, backup, no hot water, or something else.
  • Where the water or smell is, such as the basement, a bathroom, or the kitchen.
  • Whether you can reach the main shutoff and whether you already closed it.
  • What you have tried, including any chemical cleaner or plunging.
  • The age and type of the equipment if it is a heater or boiler, from the label.
  • A callback number in case the call drops.

In an emergency, do this first

Take these steps while you dial or while you wait.

Smell gas? Leave

Leave the building and move well away. Call PSE&G or 911 before you call a plumber.

Close the main valve

If water is spreading, shut off the main valve, usually near the meter or the basement wall.

Turn off power to wet areas

Only if you can reach the breaker dry. If the panel is wet, stay clear and tell the dispatcher.

Protect what you can

Lift rugs, move boxes, and put towels at the base of a gurgling floor drain.

What happens after you call

We confirm your address, explain the likely cause, and offer an appointment. Same-day visits are possible when the schedule allows, and if they are not we say so. We do not give arrival-time promises, since roads and other calls change the day.

You get a clear price before work starts. For hidden leaks or buried lines we cannot name a number until we have looked. Learn more about our plumbing services or how we work on the about page. For work in Scotch Plains, the same line applies, as described on our Scotch Plains page.

Before you dial

Have a pen or your phone notes ready. The dispatcher may ask you to read the label on a water heater or boiler, or to describe where the main shutoff valve sits. If you do not know where it is, say so, and we will help you look for it near the meter, the basement wall where the service line enters, or under the kitchen sink for individual fixtures.

If the problem is in a rented home, give the dispatcher the landlord or property manager's name and say whether you have permission to book. Tenants often need the owner's approval, and it is better to sort that out at the start of the call. If you are calling about a shop or small office, tell us the suite and the building contact.
