Frozen Pipes in Westfield, NJ: What to Do First

No water, or just a trickle, on a cold morning. The next ten minutes decide whether you thaw a pipe or repair one.

If you suspect frozen pipes in Westfield, NJ, open the faucet nearest the problem, find where the pipe is frozen, and warm that section slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels. Never use an open flame. Ice itself rarely bursts a pipe where it forms, since the damage often comes from pressure trapped between the ice and a closed faucet.

The steps below cover finding the frozen section, thawing it safely, telling a frozen pipe from a burst one, where the shutoff is, and how to prevent a repeat. The last section marks the point where thawing stops and a plumber starts.

The First Ten Minutes, in Order

Do these before warming anything. They limit damage if the pipe has already cracked.

  1. 1

    Open the faucet nearest the frozen pipe

    Open both hot and cold slightly. As the ice melts, water and pressure need somewhere to go. A dripping faucet also helps ice melt.

  2. 2

    Find the main shutoff

    Know where it is before you need it, usually near the meter or where the supply enters the basement. Check that it turns.

  3. 3

    Locate the frozen section

    Feel along exposed pipes for a cold stretch with frost on it. Pipes along exterior walls, in crawl spaces, and near basement windows freeze first.

  4. 4

    Check for cracks or bulges

    Look for splits, bulges, or damp insulation. If you see one, close the main and call. Do not thaw a pipe that already looks damaged.

  5. 5

    Warm the section slowly

    Use a hair dryer on low, warm towels, or a heating pad, working from the faucet end toward the frozen part.

Safety Rules for Thawing

Never use a torch, a propane heater, a heat gun on high, or any open flame. Soldered joints can melt, old walls and insulation can ignite, and a fire in a wall may go unseen. Never use a device that could touch standing water, and keep an extinguisher in reach if a heater is nearby.

If you cannot find the frozen spot, if it lies behind a finished wall, or if the pipe is split, stop. Let a plumber handle it.

A burst pipe often shows itself only when the ice melts and flow resumes.

If the Pipe Has Burst

Close the main shutoff, then open the lowest faucet to drain the lines. If water is near the electrical panel or an outlet, do not enter, and call 911 or the electric utility. Take photos of damage for your insurer, move belongings, and call us.

Our burst pipe repair visits begin by finding the split, drying access, and cutting out the damaged section. A pipe that froze once is a candidate for the same failure again, so we also look for the cold spot that caused it.

Prevention Before the Next Cold Snap

These steps cost little and work best before the freeze, not during it.

  • Insulate pipes in crawl spaces, garages, and along exterior walls with foam sleeves
  • Keep the heat on, even in a room you rarely use
  • Let a faucet drip on the coldest nights, especially one at the far end of an exposed run
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so room heat reaches the pipes
  • Disconnect hoses and close the interior valve to outdoor spigots
  • Seal gaps where pipes pass through walls, with caulk or foam
  • Know where your main shutoff is and test the handle each fall

When to Stop Thawing and Call

Call a plumber when the frozen section is inside a wall or under a slab, when you see a crack or bulge, when thawing has not worked after a reasonable try, or when water appears where it should not. Under New Jersey's licensing rule, cutting out a split section is licensed work.
