If You Smell Gas, Do This Before Anything Else

PSE&G guidance is to leave the building immediately and move at least 350 feet away. Then call 1-800-880-PSEG (7734) or 911. Do not use light switches, doorbells, matches, appliances, or a phone inside the house, do not try to find or fix the leak, and do not start a car in the garage.

A gas smell is never a plumber's first call. Call the utility or 911 first, and call us afterward for any plumbing work the repair needs.

Why a Boiler Locks Out or Loses Pressure

A modern condensing gas boiler runs a safety routine each time it fires. If something in that routine fails, such as a missing flame signal, low water pressure, or a blocked condensate path, the control board locks the boiler out until it is reset or the cause is fixed. A lockout is the boiler protecting itself, not a random mood.

Pressure loss is a different symptom. A sealed heating loop holds water under modest pressure, and the gauge on the front or the display shows where it sits. A slow drop usually means water is going somewhere: a weeping valve, a leaking radiator vent, or a pressure relief valve that has started to drip.

Homeowner Checks in Order, Before Anyone Opens the Cabinet

Stop at the first sign of gas, water on electrical parts, or a part you cannot name.

  1. 1

    Confirm the thermostat is calling for heat

    Set it several degrees above room temperature in heat mode and check the batteries. A blank wall control is a common cause.

  2. 2

    Check power at the boiler

    Look for a lit display. Check the switch near the unit and the breaker. A tripped breaker that trips again right away is a stop sign, not a reset-and-hope situation.

  3. 3

    Read the pressure gauge or display

    Your manual states the normal range for a cold system. A reading near zero means the loop has lost water. A reading that climbs high when hot can mean a failing expansion vessel.

  4. 4

    Look at the condensate drain

    Condensing boilers drip acidic water into a trap and a drain line. A clogged line or a frozen outdoor run can shut the boiler down. Look for a backed-up trap or ice on the outside of a drain pipe.

  5. 5

    Reset once, using the manual

    A single reset is reasonable. If it locks out again, stop. Repeated resets stress components and hide the real fault.

Carbon Monoxide: What a Homeowner Should Do

Gas heating can produce carbon monoxide when venting or burning goes wrong. This is general safety advice, not a diagnosis.

Have working CO alarms

Put them on each floor and near sleeping areas. Check the test button on a schedule and replace units on the maker's timetable.

Know the alarm response

If a CO alarm sounds, get everyone outside into fresh air and call 911. Do not reset it and go back to bed.

Watch for the quiet signs

Headaches, nausea, or dizziness that clears when you leave the house, or soot at the boiler, are reasons to shut it down and call a heating professional.

Keep vents clear

Outdoor intake and exhaust terminals must stay free of snow, leaves, and nests. Look from the ground. Do not climb to check.

When to Stop and Call

Stop and call a professional if you smell gas, if the boiler locks out again after one reset, if you see water on or near electrical parts, if the pressure relief valve is discharging, or if the system needs frequent refilling.
