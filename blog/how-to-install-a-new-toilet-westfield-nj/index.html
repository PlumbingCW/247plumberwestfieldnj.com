How to Install a New Toilet in Westfield, NJ

Setting a toilet is mostly measuring and patience. Here is the sequence, and the problems that turn it into a plumber's job.

Installing a new toilet in Westfield, NJ means shutting off the water, removing the old unit, checking the flange and floor, setting the new bowl on a fresh wax ring, bolting it down evenly, and connecting a new supply line. The flange is the ring on the floor that connects the toilet to the drain pipe, and it decides whether the job is simple or not.

This guide covers the measurements to take before you buy, what each stage of the job involves, and the conditions that make it a job for a licensed plumber. A toilet is heavy and fragile, so plan to have a second person for the lift.

Measure the Rough-In Before You Shop

Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall behind the tank to the center of the floor drain or closet bolts. Measure to the bolts, not the base of the old toilet. The most common distance is 12 inches, but 10 and 14 inch layouts exist, and a toilet built for one will not sit right on another.

Also note the bowl shape, round or elongated, and the clearance beside and in front of the toilet. Write down whether the tank bolts to the bowl or the toilet is one piece, because that affects both weight and the parts you will handle.

Check the shutoff valve behind the toilet while you are there. If the handle will not turn smoothly, you cannot isolate the water, and that is worth fixing before the day you need it in a hurry.

The Installation Sequence

Each stage has one thing that commonly goes wrong.

  1. 1

    Shut off and drain

    Close the supply valve behind the toilet, flush, and sponge out the remaining water in the tank and bowl so it is lighter and cleaner to move.

  2. 2

    Disconnect and lift

    Unscrew the supply line, remove the nuts on the closet bolts, rock the toilet gently to break the old seal, then lift straight up and set it on a towel.

  3. 3

    Inspect the flange and floor

    Scrape off the old wax. Look for a cracked or corroded flange, a flange that sits well below the floor surface, and soft or dark floor around it.

  4. 4

    Seat the new bowl on a fresh wax ring

    Place the ring on the flange or the horn of the bowl, lower the bowl straight down over the bolts, and press down evenly without twisting.

  5. 5

    Tighten the bolts in turns

    Snug each nut a little at a time, alternating sides, until the bowl is level and firm. Overtightening cracks the porcelain, so stop when the toilet no longer rocks.

  6. 6

    Mount the tank and connect the supply

    Attach the tank with its gasket and bolts, connect a new supply line, open the valve slowly and check every joint for drips.

Toilet rocks or leaks at the base?

Stop flushing it, shut the valve behind it, and have the flange and floor checked.

Pre-War Bathrooms and the State Plumbing Subcode

About 37 percent of homes in the 07090 ZIP were built in 1939 or earlier. Bathrooms in those houses often have layered flooring, an old cast iron drain stub that may have corroded, and a shutoff valve that has not moved in decades. Expect the flange to need a closer look than in a newer house, and test the shutoff gently before you rely on it.

New Jersey's plumbing subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15) adopts the 2021 edition of the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, so a finished job is expected to meet that standard. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Westfield construction office before work starts, particularly if you are moving the toilet rather than swapping it in place.

When the Job Belongs to a Licensed Plumber

New Jersey allows plumbing work to be performed only by a master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor, or their employees. A like-for-like swap is the kind of task many homeowners handle, but a failed flange, a moved drain, or any change to the waste or vent piping is not. If water has already stained the ceiling below or the floor is soft, stop and call our dispatch line before setting a new bowl.

Our toilet repair and installation visits include checking the flange and the floor first, which saves buying a toilet you cannot set.
