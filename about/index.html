This page explains what that means in practice: how a call is handled, what we do and do not do, how to check any New Jersey plumber's license, and how we handle pricing. We would rather you know the limits before you call.

How a call is handled

The dispatcher starts with safety. If you smell gas, we tell you to leave the building, move well away, and call PSE&G or 911 before anyone else. For water problems, we ask where it is coming from and whether you can reach the main shutoff, and we talk you through closing it.

Next we ask what the symptoms are. A drain that gurgles, a meter that keeps moving, or a tank that went cold each points toward different work, such as drain cleaning, leak detection, or water heater repair. We then arrange a visit, offering a same-day appointment when the schedule allows.

We do not promise an arrival time. Traffic, flooded roads, and other emergencies change the day, and a promised time we cannot keep helps nobody.

What we do and what we do not do

The limits matter as much as the services, so here they are plainly.

  • We take calls 24/7, including nights, weekends, and holidays.
  • We cover plumbing in homes and small commercial spaces, from repairs to installations. See our services page for the list.
  • We do not handle gas leaks first. Gas leaks go to PSE&G or 911, and we get involved only after the utility says the house is safe.
  • We do not quote exact numbers over the phone for hidden leaks or buried lines, because we cannot see them yet.
  • We do not do electrical, roofing, or structural work.
  • We do not pull permits for you without telling you. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Westfield construction office before work starts.
  • We do not accept requests through web forms. The line is how we work.

How to verify any New Jersey plumber

New Jersey law says plumbing work may be performed only by a master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor, or their employees. You are entitled to ask any plumber, including us, for the license number. Then confirm it with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, which is part of the Division of Consumer Affairs.

Master plumber licenses expire every two years on June 30 of odd-numbered years, so ask whether the license is current. A plumber who cannot or will not give you a number is a reason to stop and call someone else. This applies to every company, not just ours.

How we handle pricing

You get a clear price before work starts. We explain the factors that move cost: how deep or hidden the problem is, how easy the pipe or fixture is to reach, the materials involved, whether a permit applies, and what time of day the call comes in. Installing a new heater is a different job from clearing a stoppage, for example, which is why water heater replacement and a simple fixture fix are quoted separately.

If the right answer is to replace rather than repair, we say so and explain why. If the repair is cheap and the replacement would be wasteful, we say that too. The decision is yours.

What we ask of you

Know where your main water shutoff is before you need it. Take a photo of the label on your water heater. If you rent, tell the landlord about a leak early, since slow seepage rarely shows up on a lease. Homeowners policies often cover sudden accidental discharge but not wear or slow seepage, so read yours and photograph damage before cleanup.
