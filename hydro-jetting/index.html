Hydro jetting in Westfield, NJ is the cleaning of a drain or sewer line with a high-pressure water hose and a nozzle that fires jets backward to scour the pipe wall and pull itself forward. It removes grease, soap scale, silt and small roots instead of poking a channel through them. It is a cleaning method, not a repair, and on old pipe it starts with a camera.

Cable or jet: what each one leaves behind

Cabling (snaking)

  • Spins a cutting head through the clog and leaves a channel the width of the head
  • Good for a fresh, soft or hair-and-paper stoppage
  • Grease and scale stay stuck to the pipe wall
  • Quick to set up and gentle on thin or fragile pipe
  • The clog often returns in weeks if the wall stays coated

Hydro jetting

  • Washes the full inside of the pipe with a rotating spray
  • Good for grease, scale, silt and repeat clogs
  • Carries debris downstream and out instead of leaving it
  • Needs a clear path, a stable pipe and a camera look first
  • Cannot fix a break, a collapse or a sag that holds water

What the water does to grease, roots and scale

Grease is the easiest to explain. The force of the spray breaks a thick wall of kitchen fat into pieces small enough to flush. A cable cannot do that, which is why a grease-lined kitchen drain gets better results from a jetting pass.

Roots enter a line through joints and hairline cracks and grow into dense mats. A nozzle with a forward-facing jet clears fine root hair and thinner strands, while the rotating spray scrubs the pipe wall behind it. The cut roots will regrow if the joint still leaks, so jetting buys time rather than ending the problem.

Scale is a mineral and rust crust on cast iron and galvanized steel. The spray knocks off loose scale and exposes the pipe underneath, and the flakes come out at the cleanout. If a lot of metal comes out, we say so and suggest a look at the wall thickness with the camera.

Signs your line is a jetting job

These patterns point away from a one-time clog and toward buildup on the pipe wall.

The same drain clogs every few weeks

The wall coating is narrowing the pipe again after each cable pass.

Slow drains in more than one fixture

A partly narrowed branch or main line backs up under heavy use, like a laundry day.

Gurgling after a cable visit

Air is moving through a pipe that is still partly blocked.

Foul smell from a floor drain

Grease and silt rot in the pipe wall and in the trap seal.

Rust or root threads on a cable

The line has scale or a root problem that a cable only trims.

What jetting cannot fix

A bellied pipe is a section that has sunk and holds a pool of water and waste. Jetting can clean the belly but cannot lift it, so debris settles there again. A collapsed pipe is worse: the nozzle cannot pass and the water has nowhere to go.

Those findings move you to sewer line repair, which covers spot fixes, relining and replacement. If your last cleaning turned up a dip or a break, call the dispatch line and tell us what you saw. We would rather say a cleaning is short-term than sell one that cannot last.

Backed up right now? What to do before the jetter is set up

Jetting equipment needs a clear path and a calm work area. These steps help.

Stop all water use

No flushing, laundry or dishwasher cycles. Water you add will find the lowest drain and come up there.

Find the cleanout

A capped fitting near the foundation or the basement floor. Clear a four-foot area around it so the hose can be set up.

Move valuables from floor drains

Raise boxes, rugs and electronics off the basement floor. If water is already coming up, leave it for us to deal with and stay out of standing water near outlets.

Note what has been tried

Tell us if you used chemical cleaner or a plunger and when the line last had service. It changes the plan.

The jetting sequence, from camera to final check

  1. 1

    Camera inspection

    A push camera goes in through the cleanout so we can see wall condition, joints and the location of the blockage.

  2. 2

    Select the nozzle

    A cutting nozzle for roots, a rotating scouring nozzle for grease and scale, and a penetrating nozzle for a hard plug.

  3. 3

    Set pressure in stages

    We start low and raise it as the pipe allows. Old or thin pipe gets a gentler pass.

  4. 4

    Run in both directions

    The nozzle advances with the hose, then is pulled back at a slower pace so it scrubs the walls on the return.

  5. 5

    Re-inspect and test

    A second camera pass confirms the wall is clean and water runs freely, and we run fixtures to test the flow.

  • Length and size of the lineLonger runs and larger pipe need more hose, more water and more time on site.

  • Type and amount of buildupSoft grease, packed roots and old scale respond differently and take different time.
