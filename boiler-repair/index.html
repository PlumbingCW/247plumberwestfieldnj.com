Boiler repair in Westfield, NJ covers hot water and steam boilers that stop heating, leak, lose pressure, short cycle, or lock out on a fault. When the house is cold, that is an emergency, and our dispatch line is open 24/7 for it. While you wait, check the thermostat and the boiler's power switch, and keep the heat in with doors closed and a space heater only if you can run one safely.

A boiler job is part plumbing and part heating controls, and we are straight about which part we do. We handle the water side: pressure, feed valves, relief valves, expansion tanks, circulators, zone valves, leaks, and piping. Burner, gas train, and oil burner work is done by licensed heating contractors, and we will say so on the phone.

No heat right now: five checks before you call

These take a few minutes and sometimes end the problem. Do not open the boiler cabinet.

  • Thermostat set to heat, set several degrees above room temperature, with fresh batteries if it takes them
  • The boiler's emergency switch (a red plate or a switch near the stairs) is on, and the breaker has not tripped
  • The pressure gauge reads in the normal range on the dial, not near zero and not near the top
  • Fuel supply: gas appliances on, or oil level above empty on the tank gauge
  • A single press of the reset button if the manual allows it. If it locks out again, stop and call

Gas smell, fuel oil smell, or soot: leave, then call

If you smell gas, PSE&G's guidance is to leave the building immediately, move at least 350 feet away, and call 1-800-880-PSEG (7734) or 911. Do not use light switches, doorbells, matches, appliances, or a phone inside, and do not try to find the leak. Call us only after the house is cleared.

A strong fuel oil smell, black soot on or around the boiler, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounding also means get everyone outside and call 911. Never restart a boiler that has sooted or shown flame rolling out of the burner.

While you wait for the technician: four things to do

The aim is to protect the pipes and give the visit a head start.

Keep the pipes from freezing

In a deep cold snap, open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls and let a faucet drip. A frozen line in a cold house turns a no-heat call into a burst pipe.

Note the gauges and the display

Write down the pressure, the temperature, and any fault code. Take a photo of the boiler's data plate.

Do not bleed or drain on a hunch

Opening valves randomly can drop pressure or flood the floor. Close the water feed valve only if water is actively leaking.

Clear the area

Make room around the boiler and the nearest radiators or baseboard so the technician can reach every valve.

Hot water boilers and steam boilers fail differently

A hot water boiler heats water and pushes it through a closed loop of baseboard, radiators, or in-floor tubing. It runs under moderate pressure, controlled by a fill valve and an expansion tank, so the common faults are pressure faults. Low pressure suggests a leak or a stuck fill valve, and high pressure after heating suggests a waterlogged expansion tank.

A steam boiler boils water to make steam that rises to radiators and then condenses back down. Its faults are about the water level, not the pressure, since the pressure is only a pound or two. A failing low water cutoff, a dirty sight glass, or a clogged radiator vent produces banging, uneven heat, or a shutdown. The two systems look similar from the street and are nothing alike inside.

Short cycling, lockouts, and pressure swings

Short cycling means the boiler fires, shuts off in a couple of minutes, and fires again. The cause may be a boiler too large for the house, a thermostat in a draft, a zone with a stuck valve that holds back flow, or an aquastat set too close to its limit. Each wears the burner and wastes fuel.

A lockout is the boiler saying it detected a fault and will not try again until reset. Resetting once is reasonable. If it locks out again, something is wrong with ignition, flame sensing, or venting, and repeated resets only stress the equipment. Call the dispatch line and tell us the code. Annual boiler service: what actually gets checked

A yearly visit before the heating season catches faults while they are still cheap to fix. On the water side we test the relief valve, check the expansion tank pressure, inspect the fill valve, and bleed air from the loops. A stuck relief valve or dead expansion tank will fail on the first cold week if nobody has touched it since spring.

Burner cleaning and combustion testing belong to the heating contractor, and a good annual service includes both. If you want one visit to cover both, say so when you call and we will explain how to line them up. Annual service also follows the maker's published service guidelines for your boiler model. New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code. A replacement or new piping run may need a permit, so ask the Westfield construction office.

What drives the cost of a boiler repair

A boiler repair can be a small valve or a pump swap, and it can be a new boiler. We cannot name a figure before we have seen it, but we give a clear price before any work starts, and we tell you if the right answer is a contractor.

  • The part that failedA feed valve or relief valve is a smaller job than a circulator, a zone valve manifold, or a section of leaking main.
  • System type and layoutSteam systems, multiple zones, and boilers in cramped utility rooms take more time.
  • Age and condition of the boilerA boiler near the end of its life may need more than one repair, so the conversation shifts to replacement.
  • Draining and refillingSome repairs mean draining part of the system and refilling it, treating the water, and purging air.
  • After-hours timingA cold-night emergency visit is scheduled differently from a booked daytime visit.

A burst heating line that floods a room may be covered as a sudden event, but a boiler that simply wore out usually is not, so read the policy. If the system is down tonight, call (908) 357-1542 and tell us what the gauges show.
