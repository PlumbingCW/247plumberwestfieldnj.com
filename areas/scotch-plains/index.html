Urgent plumbing help across the township, any hour

Emergency service means a person answers, at 3 a.m. or 3 p.m., and starts working out what to shut off. Here are the calls we hear most from Scotch Plains homes.

Basement water

Seepage along the wall, a sump that does not kick on, or a floor drain that bubbles. See sump pump installation and repair for what we check.

Backed-up drains

Tub, toilet, and washer slowing together means the main line is struggling. A crew can clear it with drain cleaning once we know where it sits.

Sewer line trouble

A line packed with roots or grease needs sewer line cleaning before water finds the lowest drain in the house.

Hidden leaks

A meter that keeps turning with every tap off suggests water escaping. Leak detection finds it with listening gear before walls are opened.

Failed water heater

A tank leaking at the base or a burner that will not hold calls for water heater repair or a swap, and the cold supply valve above it stops the water.

Before we arrive: what to do now

These steps limit damage while the dispatcher lines up a plumber. They take a few minutes.

Close the main valve

If you have a burst pipe or an active leak, shut off the main water valve, usually near the meter or where the line enters the basement. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near outlets, appliances, or a panel, switch off the breaker for that area only if you can reach it dry. If the panel itself is wet, stay away and tell us on the call.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes, and anything you can lift off the floor. Keep children and pets out of standing water.

Call the 24/7 line

Call the dispatch line and tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from. If you smell gas, leave the house and call PSE&G or 911 before anyone else.

Ida, July 2025, and what Scotch Plains basements learned

Hurricane Ida on September 1, 2021 brought upwards of 10 inches of rain to Scotch Plains. Portions of Route 22 closed, and West Broad Street, Westfield Road, Lamberts Mill Road, Rahway and Raritan Roads, Glenside Avenue, Manitou Way, and apartments near the Clark border flooded. Water reached the front steps of the municipal building on Park Avenue, and firefighters evacuated more than 100 people.

The township's police logged 185 storm-related calls, including 53 stranded vehicles, and flooded basements were reported across town. For a homeowner, that history is a reason to test a sump pump before the rain and to know where your floor drain connects. A pump with no battery backup stops when the power goes, and several hundred customers lost power in Ida.

A July 2025 storm reached 7.9 inches in Scotch Plains according to The Leader. Robinson's Branch of the Rahway River runs through the township toward the Robinson's Branch Reservoir, so low spots near the stream can fill quickly when rain arrives faster than the ground absorbs it. If you live near it, ask about basement waterproofing before the next storm season rather than after.

Sewage backing up through a basement floor drain during a heavy storm is a separate risk, because sewers can fill when stormwater gets in. A backflow valve may be an option on the line, but whether one is required or allowed is a question for the township construction office.
