Sewer Camera Inspection in Westfield, NJ

A camera on a flexible rod shows what is actually inside your sewer line, so a buying, repair or cleaning decision rests on footage, not a guess.

A sewer camera inspection in Westfield, NJ is a recorded look inside the pipe between your house and the street, made by pushing a waterproof camera head down the line from a cleanout. It shows the pipe material, joints, cracks, roots, sags and buildup as they are today. The footage becomes the evidence for what, if anything, needs doing.

It is the quiet job on our list. You schedule it before a closing, after a pattern of clogs, or when you want a baseline on an older home. Urgent backups are handled by our 24/7 dispatch line, but a planned inspection can usually be booked for a day that suits you.

What you get: footage, a location and a plain-English read

You are not paying for a walk around the basement. You are paying for a video of the inside of the pipe, a mark on the ground showing where the problem sits and how deep, and an explanation of what it means. If the camera finds nothing, that is a useful result too.

When a camera inspection makes sense

These are the situations where we most often suggest putting a camera in the line.

  • Before you buy a house, especially one built before 1960
  • A clog that returns after cabling or jetting
  • A backup after a heavy storm or flooding event
  • Gurgling in the lowest drains or a sewer smell in the basement
  • Planning a tree planting, driveway, or addition near the line
  • A slow drain on the main line with no visible cause
  • After a repair or lining job, to confirm the work

How the inspection goes on site

  1. 1

    Locate the access

    We find the cleanout, or pull a toilet or open a vent if there is none. We tell you which before we begin.

  2. 2

    Feed the camera

    The head is pushed down the line on a flexible rod, with lights and a cable that measures how far it has travelled.

  3. 3

    Watch and record

    We watch the live feed, note distances to each feature and save the video.

  4. 4

    Locate and mark

    A transmitter in the camera head lets a receiver above ground find the exact spot and depth. We mark the ground or note landmarks.

  5. 5

    Walk through the findings

    We review the footage with you and give you the distances, depths and what each finding means.

A push camera: what it can and cannot show

What it shows

  • Pipe material and joint style where visible
  • Cracks, offsets, roots and scale on the walls
  • Sags and bellies where water pools
  • Distance and depth to each finding
  • A recording you can keep and share

What it cannot do

  • See through a fully blocked line (the line must be cleared first)
  • Judge pipe strength from the video alone
  • Show the whole house plumbing, only the line it travels
  • Pass some tight bends or severely collapsed sections
  • Tell you the cause of a problem without context from you

How to read a sewer camera report

A good report lists each finding with a distance from the cleanout, a short description and a still image or time stamp. Distances matter because they let a repair crew find the spot. Ask for the video file as well as the notes.

Not every finding is a repair. A few light roots at a joint or minor scale is a maintenance item. A crack with root intrusion, a clear offset or a belly with standing water is a decision point. If we cannot see a section, the report should say so, and you should ask what that section is likely to hold.

What affects the cost of a camera inspection

We cannot name a number before seeing the access and the line length, and we give you a clear price before work starts. A call to the dispatch line gets you booked, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

  • Access to the lineA cleanout makes the job quick. Pulling a toilet or opening a vent adds time.
  • Length and bendsA longer run with several turns takes more time to travel and to record.
  • Cleaning needed firstA line blocked with debris or grease has to be cleared before the camera can see it.
  • Locating and markingFinding and marking a depth and a path across a yard adds time compared with a view-only run.
  • Report detailA short verbal review is different from a written report with distances and stills.
