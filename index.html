Not sure who to call first? Start with what you see

Most callers describe a symptom, not a trade. This is how we sort the common ones when the phone rings.

Water spreading on a floor

Close the main valve, switch off power to wet areas, and call. A supply leak keeps pushing water until it is shut off, so the valve comes first.

Toilet, tub and sink all slow

Several fixtures slowing together points past the branch lines to the main drain. Stop using water and call for drain cleaning before the lowest drain in the house backs up.

Cold showers, or a tank puddle

A cold tank with a pilot or breaker that checks out, or water pooling at the base, needs a plumber. Shut the cold supply valve above the tank if it is wet.

Smell of gas

Leave the building, move well away, and call PSE&G or 911 first. A gas leak is not a plumbing call until the utility says the house is safe.

Water bill jumped, nothing visible

A hidden leak under a slab or behind a wall leaves no puddle. We start with the meter test and a listening tool, then narrow it down.

What a 24/7 dispatch line does for a Westfield homeowner

Plumbing problems rarely wait for business hours. Pipes burst when the house is coldest, water heaters fail on the morning of a holiday, and drains back up on the night the whole family is home. Having one number that answers in the middle of the night means you are not scrolling through listings with a wet floor.

When you call, the dispatcher asks where the water is, whether you can reach the shutoff, and what has already been tried. That short conversation decides whether you need leak detection, a rooter visit, or a heater technician, and it tells you what to do before anyone arrives. Call the dispatch line and we will walk you through the shutoff over the phone if you have never touched it.

Same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows, and we say plainly when it does not. We do not promise an arrival time we cannot control, because traffic on Route 22 or a flooded road changes everything.

From the first ring to a fixed pipe

Here is what actually happens after you dial, in order.

  1. 1

    You describe the problem

    The dispatcher asks where the water or smell is, what shut off, and whether anyone is in danger. If it is gas, we send you to PSE&G or 911 first.

  2. 2

    We give you something to do right away

    That might be closing the main valve, shutting a heater's cold supply, or turning off the breaker to a wet room. These steps limit damage while you wait.

  3. 3

    A plumber arrives and diagnoses

    The plumber checks the symptom with the right tool, such as a pressure gauge, a moisture meter, or a drain camera, and explains the cause in plain words.

  4. 4

    You get a clear price before work starts

    We explain the cost factors and the options. If the answer is to replace rather than repair, you hear why, and you decide.

  5. 5

    The repair, then a test

    We fix the fault, run water, check for leaks and drainage, and tell you what to watch over the next few days.

When a repair stops making sense

A tank that leaks from its seams cannot be patched, because the leak is in the steel liner behind the insulation. A sewer line with roots that return every few months may have a cracked joint that cabling only delays. We tell you which side of that line your problem sits on.

For drains that keep coming back, we may suggest a sewer camera inspection before cutting anything open, since the picture shows whether the pipe is bellied, broken, or just coated. Cost depends on access, depth, material, and whether permits are needed, so we explain those factors on the phone. For permit rules, ask the Westfield construction office before work starts.

A water heater swap or a new line is covered by New Jersey's plumbing subcode, which adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code.
